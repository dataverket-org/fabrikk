# New terms

*Part of [the story](../README.md).* The first story of the control plane's own: **a provider replaces the terms of a product its customers are using; every organisation that accepted the old terms is told and given notice, most accept the new terms and keep the product, one does not and its terms expire, and the provider ends what that organisation held of the product at the end of notice.** Terms follow [the glossary](../../language/README.md); what the story added to the model is listed at the end.

## At a glance

- **It starts** when the provider's operator publishes new terms for a product that organisations already hold resources from, and **ends** when every one of them holds the product under the new terms or has been offboarded from it under the old.
- **Two pivotal events** of the product's lifecycle under an agreement, neither the agreement's: *New terms published*, after which two terms are listed and a clock runs for every organisation that accepted the old ones; and *Terms expired*, after which that organisation may ask for no more of the product.
- **Nothing is withheld by an expiry.** What is held stays held and reachable under the terms it was taken under; the provider ends it only by offboarding of the product, with that product's notice. An expiry is one product's and no sanction.
- **The provider chooses when to end the product of an organisation whose terms expired,** or whether to at all; an expiry starts no clock of its own. The owner's decision.
- **Pending terms are replaced, never withdrawn.** A provider that changes its mind publishes again, the current terms' content included; organisations that had accepted the replaced pending terms are replaced again and told again.

## The events

The story as it would run in a provider's business today, with contracts and email; the system words are kept out.

1. New terms drafted *(the provider's own work, outside the story: its lawyers, its board, the cooperative's model terms)*
2. Terms published *(by the operator, for one product, with an effective date; the notice is the terms being replaced's own promise of notice, and keeping to it is the provider's legal matter, not a rule of the control plane, which carries the date)*
3. New terms published *(the catalogue lists the current terms and the pending ones with their date; every organisation that accepted the old terms is told, as of everything concerning what it holds, and sees the date to accept by)*
4. Then, for each such organisation, one of:
   - Terms accepted *(again, by an owner, naming the new terms, before the date: the organisation holds the product under them from the date, and its asks continue unbroken)*
   - Terms expired *(the date passed with no acceptance: the product refuses the organisation's new asks from then on; what it holds stays held and reachable under the old terms; the owner is told)*
   - Terms accepted *(after the expiry, by an owner: the expiry is lifted and asks resume; nothing held was touched)*
5. Offboarding requested *(by the provider's operator, for the product, under the old terms, with reason terms expired, for an organisation it will not keep on the old terms; with that product's notice)*
6. The product ends what the organisation held of it at the end of notice, since the organisation will not
7. Organisation cleared, then Terms ended *(the organisation's terms for the product end; the account is no longer wired for it; the agreement stands with its other products)*
8. Terms published again, before the date *(the provider replaces pending terms with others, or with the current terms' content to call the change off: every organisation that had accepted the replaced pending terms is replaced again, with the new date, and told again)*

Side paths: an organisation offboarding the product is told and cannot accept, since the product's state never goes backwards; an organisation that takes the product during the window accepts the old terms or the new, and if the old, is replaced at once with the date.

## People and parties

The provider's operator publishes and, later, requests the offboarding; the owner accepts; the editor sees only that asks are refused after an expiry, and gives back if the owner decides to leave the product. No outside party: the terms' content is between the provider and its lawyers.

## Hotspots, resolved

### Can pending terms be withdrawn?

No; replaced only, so that one mechanism covers a change of mind, a correction and a second change, and an organisation that accepted early is handled the same way each time. *The facilitator's resolution, confirmed by the owner.*

### Does an expiry start the offboarding by itself?

No; the provider's operator requests it, when it chooses, so that a provider may keep an organisation on old terms for as long as it likes. The alternative, an expiry that is itself the start of the product's offboarding with the terms' notice, is one state and one command fewer and takes the choice away. *Decided by the owner: the provider chooses.*

### Who sees the old terms afterwards?

Audit holds the acceptance and the publication as entries, by id; the terms' text is the catalogue's and the provider's to keep, under the record's retention. An organisation that wants the words it accepted keeps them itself, as any customer keeps a contract.
