# Giving the example product back

*Part of [the story](../README.md).* The sixth story from a person's side: **an owner decides the organisation no longer wants the example product; the editors give the resources back, or the product ends what is still held at the end of the terms' notice; the product answers cleared, the terms end, and the agreement with the provider stands with nothing in it.** Terms follow [the glossary](../../language/README.md); what the story added to the model is listed at the end.

## At a glance

- **It starts** when an owner requests offboarding of the example product, and **ends** when the organisation's terms for it have ended and the agreement stands empty.
- **Every offboarding carries the terms' notice, whoever requested it.** An owner's request for a product runs the same notice as the provider's: the editors give back within it, and what is still held at its end the product ends, as it does for the provider's request. The owner who wants it faster gives back sooner. The owner's decision, which closes the question left open since the cut, and one rule where there were two.
- **The notice is an upper bound, never a wait.** A product's terms end on *Organisation cleared*, which the product answers as soon as nothing is held and nothing reserved, at once if nothing was; at the end of notice it ends what is still held and answers then. The owner's decision.
- **The agreement stands with nothing in it,** until the product is taken again, [back and again](back-and-again.md), or the agreement itself is offboarded, by the owners or by the provider's operator for dormancy.
- **No pivotal event of the agreement's.** *Terms ended* for one product is the product's lifecycle's end under the agreement, which runs again on a later *Terms accepted*.

## The events

The story as it would run in a provider's business today: a customer cancels one service, with a month's notice.

1. Offboarding requested *(by an owner, for the example product, with the terms' notice; the product's acceptance refuses new asks of the organisation and releases its reservations; the editors are told, and the day the notice ends)*
2. Resource given back, once per resource *(each a *give back* by an editor, within the notice; a resource an editor cannot yet give back is refused with the reason)*
3. The end of notice, if resources are still held *(the product ends what is held, as for the provider's request; the organisation is told of each)*
4. Organisation cleared *(the product's answer, as soon as nothing is held and nothing reserved: before the notice's end when the editors finished, at its end otherwise)*
5. Terms ended *(the organisation's terms for the product; the account is no longer wired for it; the product's entry under the agreement is ended; the agreement stands)*
6. The product deletes what it kept of the organisation's *(its own rule, under the terms; the audit entries stay in the provider's record)*

Side paths: an owner who changes their mind cannot accept the product's terms while it is offboarding, and takes it again after *Terms ended*, as if for the first time; an owner who offboards the agreement itself names every product it holds, each with its own notice, and the agreement ends when the last has; a product given back with nothing held ends at once, the notice having nothing to wait for.

## People and parties

The owner, who requests; the editors, who give back; the example product, which refuses, ends and answers; the provider's operator, who sees nothing of it but the record. No outside party.

## Hotspots

### Who ends what is still held when the owner asked?

The product, at the end of the terms' notice, as for the provider's request: an owner's offboarding carries the notice too. The alternatives, nobody, so that a product left with resources stays offboarding for ever, and an owner naming a day on the request, were not chosen: the first leaves an organisation holding and, once money returns, paying without end, the second is two shapes of one command. *Decided by the owner,* in place of the earlier rule that the provider ends only what it was asked to end.

### When do the terms end?

On *Organisation cleared*, whenever the product answers it, the notice being an upper bound and never a wait; a product with nothing held ends at once. The alternative, always at the end of notice, was not chosen: a pointless wait when nothing is held. *Decided by the owner.*

### Is the owners' closing of the organisation the same rule?

Yes: the installation's agreement holds nothing but the organisation, so its offboarding by the owners ends at once, which is this rule with nothing to wait for; the operator's dormancy request runs the notice because the organisation has not asked. *The facilitator's resolution.*
