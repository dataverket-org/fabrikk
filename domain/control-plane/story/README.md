# The story

The control plane's story is the part of the stormed story that would be the same whatever a provider's products deliver: a person logs in at the installation's front desk, accepts its terms, validates the login and creates an organisation, which holds an agreement with the installation from its first moment; the organisation comes to a provider it has no relationship with by accepting one of its products' terms, is accountable to it, holds things from its products, gives products back, and leaves, the installation last. The things themselves belong to each product's own story; here a product is *the example product*, with only what the control plane can see of any product, and a thing held is *a resource*. The installation's own product is *the control plane*, which every organisation holds. Terms follow the [glossary](../language/README.md).

These chapters are what was found to hold for any product. They are a hypothesis until a real product confirms them, and [the method](../../method/README.md) says what that means. Stories of the control plane's own, with the example product's facts as their edges, are stormed one at a time and listed under contents; each adds to the chapters, the gates and the designs, and says what it added.

## At a glance

- **The story describes the world, not software.** An event belongs on the board if it would happen in a provider's business today, with spreadsheets and email.
- **It starts** when someone with a login arrives at the installation's front desk, wanting an organisation of their own, first or second.
- **It ends** when the organisation's data is deleted; retained records end later still.
- **Three principles** are the control plane's: organisations govern themselves and share nothing, records are kept as long as the law requires, and an organisation is told of everything concerning what it holds. They are design constraints, not aspirations.
- **Three pivotal events,** *Organisation created*, *Agreement activated* and *Agreement ended*. Before each one set of things is true, and after it another.

## Pivotal events

| Event | What changes |
|---|---|
| **Organisation created** | A visitor becomes an owner answerable for an organisation, with an account, a record and an agreement with the installation, born with it and never absent after |
| **Agreement activated** | A stranger becomes someone the provider will serve. Before it, nothing can be asked of the provider's products; after it, the organisation is accountable to the provider. It follows the first *Terms accepted* once every condition for activation the provider's policy names is met, at once where it names none |
| **Agreement ended** | The organisation's agreement with the provider ends; after the last, which is always the installation's, only deletion and retention remain |

A product has pivotal events of its own, on the journey of the thing it delivers; they are its story's, not this one's. Boundaries tend to fall at or near pivotal events.
