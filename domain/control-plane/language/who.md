# Who's who

*Part of [the glossary](README.md).* Each entry says what the word means; the rules behind it are in the page the entry links to.

| Term | Norwegian | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| **Person** | — | A real human, proven by a means of validation and known inside the system by a *person id* that the installation's Person mints and that nothing else can resolve. The external ID the validation reveals, the national identity number, is used only to recognise the same human at the next validation and never leaves Person. One person id per human across the installation, so two organisations' records naming it is the one correlation kept on purpose. Rules: [Person and Organisation](../map/person-and-organisation.md). |
| **Login** | — | What a person logs in with, from any identity provider the installation allows; the identity provider's, not the control plane's. A login spans organisations: it may have a user in several, and is validated once for all of them. Not a word of the story's events. |
| **User** | — | A login's membership of exactly one organisation, with roles; covered by the organisation's agreement with the installation, validated when its login is, and deleted with the organisation. Audited under the person id where its login is validated and under its user id where not. |
| **Validated** | — | The state of a login whose human has been proven through a means of validation, BankID or Vipps, which reveals the national identity number; carried by every user of that login. A login through FEIDE, Entra ID or Google is not validation. Validation is once per login, before which the login has accepted the installation's terms, and Person tells every organisation the login has a user in, *Login validated*. Rules: [Person and Organisation](../map/person-and-organisation.md). |
| **Visitor** | — | A login at the installation's front desk, with or without organisations elsewhere: it reads the catalogue, accepts the installation's terms, validates and creates an organisation. It holds no role; what the front desk requires of it is the installation organisation's policy. A login with no user in any organisation is dropped after the installation's dormancy period for visitors, *Visitor dropped*. Rules: [the front desk](system-words.md) and [the first message](../design/tenancy/first-message.md). |
| **Provider** | — | An organisation offering products to other organisations under agreements. Any organisation becomes one by announcing a product into the catalogue with its terms published beside it, and stops being one when it announces none. The installation's organisation is one, of the control plane; in the stories one more is, of the example product. Its say over a customer: the conditions for activation its policy names, the quota, new terms with notice, and offboarding under the terms. What it does to organisations is done under the operator duty. |
| **Installation** | — | One control plane and everything that answers to it: the organisation that owns it, its front desk and its catalogue, and every organisation created at that front desk, the providers among them. Known by its organisation, and the edge of what a login's validation, a person id and the catalogue reach. Place is no part of it: an installation may serve one datacenter or several, and a product's announcement says where each item is offered. |
| **The installation's organisation** | — | Whichever organisation owns the installation's account and the front desk; nothing else tells it apart from a provider. It runs the identity provider, the messaging system, admission, Tenancy, Person, the catalogue and the installation's Audit, and provides the control plane to every organisation, with which each holds an agreement from its creation. Given before the story, and the one organisation with no agreement with the installation, since an agreement is between two organisations. Its organisation policy is what the front desk enforces and names the installation's two dormancy periods, for visitors and for organisations. Where a page has "the installation" act as a party, it is shorthand for this organisation; the whole is the *installation*. |
| **Organisation** | — | A team or project, sharing nothing with other organisations. Created at the front desk by a validated visitor, born with its agreement with the installation, and never without an agreement: the installation's is the last to end, and giving it back is closing the organisation. The party that holds things from products, and the party the products' facts name. |
| **Agreement** | Kundeforhold | An organisation's relationship with one provider: the products it has taken, each with its terms accepted and its quota, and whether it is held, active, offboarding or ended. One per provider, the installation's among them, born at *Organisation created*; any other born when an owner first accepts one of the provider's products' terms. Active once every condition for activation the provider's policy names is met. Ended by offboarding of the agreement. Rules: [Organisation's design](../design/organisation/README.md). |
| **Condition for activation** | — | See [words beneath the story](system-words.md). |
| **Offboarding** | — | Giving a product back, or leaving a provider: requested for one product or for an agreement, by an owner by choice or by the provider's operator under the terms with a reason. Every request carries the terms' notice; the product answers *Organisation cleared* when it holds nothing, and the terms end on the answer. For the installation's agreement it is how an organisation closes, under the organisation policy's closing rule, or how the installation ends a dormant one. Rules: [Organisation's commands](../design/organisation/commands.md) and [policies](../design/organisation/policies.md); the product's side in [the seam](../../method/seam.md). |
| **Owner** | — | A user's role that commits the organisation: accepts terms, onboards users, grants roles, sets the organisation policy, requests and withdraws offboarding. Always a user whose login is validated, the floor the system fixes; held by one to N users, and at least one enabled owner always exists. |
| **Editor** | — | A user's role that operates what the organisation holds: the *ask*, *operate* and *give back* command classes, narrowed per product by the organisation policy if it says so. |
| **Viewer** | — | A user's role that reads what the organisation holds and changes nothing: the *read* command class alone. |
| **Organisation policy** | — | The rules an organisation sets for itself, written in the system's vocabulary so that a policy means the same at every provider: which roles require a validated login and by which means; whether departing users are disabled or deleted; who may do what with what it holds, by role or user, product and command class; the closing rule, any owner or every enabled owner; and, for a provider, which users hold the operator duty and which conditions for activation its agreements wait on. A product's own commands never appear in it. Always written in full: the bare word *policy* is Stack's. Rules: [Organisation's commands](../design/organisation/commands.md), *set policy*. |
| **Duty** | — | What a provider's user must hold to act on an organisation or on the catalogue: the operator, the one duty, held only as the provider's organisation policy grants it and read at authorisation for *approve agreement*, *set quota*, *publish terms*, *withdraw product* and *request offboarding*. Not a role: roles are what a user does with its own organisation's things. |
| **Customer** | Kunde | Informal word for an organisation. Fine in conversation and on the website; use "organisation" in events, code and definitions. |
