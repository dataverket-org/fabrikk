# The provider is a tenant of its own

*Part of [the design decisions](README.md).* The domain owner's intent from the start.

## Context

Every instance pins one tenant at startup and rejects any message whose tenant differs; that is what makes isolation structural. Several contexts serve every organisation at once: Catalogue is read by strangers, Audit holds everything, Person spans organisations, and a product's provider-side contexts serve every organisation from what the provider holds. Admission is the one unpinned instance; these would be more.

## Decision

The provider is an organisation in its own right, with its own account, and so is the installation's organisation, the one that runs the installation. A provider's Audit and a product's provider-side contexts pin the provider tenant; Person, Catalogue and the installation's Audit pin the installation organisation's tenant. Messages between an organisation's contexts and a provider's, or the installation's, cross accounts only through explicit exports and imports. The rule that every instance pins one tenant stands, with three readings: an organisation-pinned instance accepts only a message naming its own organisation; a provider-pinned instance accepts a message naming any organisation that has an agreement with this provider, and only one that arrived on that organisation's declared export; an instance pinned to the installation's organisation accepts a message naming any organisation of the installation, arrived on its export or from the front desk. The account layer guarantees the arrival. The envelope's organisation is the organisation the fact concerns, never the sender's account; the import tells who sent it. The envelope's actor is likewise what admission bound the sender's connection to, never what the sender wrote.

Rejected: an unpinned class of provider-side instances carrying each message's tenant through without the account wall, as admission does. It is simpler to wire, but the isolation guarantee would then hold per handler rather than by construction. The provider-side instances do carry each message's organisation; what makes that safe is that the message could only have arrived on that organisation's export. It remains the fallback if the export wiring proves heavy, which the first slice can measure. Also rejected: a provider-side instance per organisation, which contradicts what a provider-side context is for, serving every organisation from what the provider holds.

## Consequences

- **A crossing belongs to the receiver once it has crossed.** What has crossed into a provider's or the installation's account stays there whether the organisation's account exists or not, so an organisation's account may go as soon as its last message has crossed, and Audit, which must hold every message, is the party that has it: the installation Audit's *Record closed* is what Tenancy deletes on.
- The crossing events in [the map](../map/README.md) between an organisation's contexts, Organisation and a product's organisation-side contexts, and the provider's or the installation's are the account exports and imports. Nothing new is invented; each is declared in [the integration contract](integration-contract.md).
- **A party's acts are recorded by the party it is accountable to,** the rule stated once in [the audit decision](audit.md): an organisation's acts within an agreement by that agreement's provider, the installation among them, and a provider's own acts by the installation, as one more declared export.
- "An organisation sees every audit entry about what it holds" is an export from each Audit's account back to each organisation's: one entry per organisation it concerns, naming that organisation alone, delivered as it happens. The record is append-only, enforced by the installation, and a provider sits in an account it does not operate; see [Audit](../map/audit.md).
- **A provider organisation is created at the front desk and owned like any other,** and becomes a provider by announcing a product into the catalogue with its terms beside it. Its users hold the three roles, and the operator duty only as its organisation policy grants it; every command it sends onto an organisation is authorised against the duty. It holds the installation's agreement as every organisation does, and may hold one with another provider as a customer, which [two lifecycles](two-lifecycles.md) allows.
- **An installation may hold several provider organisations.** Each is an organisation with its own account and its own pinned instances of the provider-side contexts, and all announce into the one catalogue. The rule that every instance pins one tenant scales to N providers by running N instances; nothing here assumes one provider beside the installation, only that each provider-side instance serves exactly one.
- **This story has one provider of products beside the installation.** An organisation chooses a provider by accepting one of its products' terms; the installation it does not choose. The domain owner intends a cooperative of several providers; the shape allows it, one agreement and one wiring per provider.
- **Person and Catalogue pin the installation organisation's tenant, and the visitor stands at the front desk,** an account of the installation's that is no tenant's, which exports *accept installation terms*, *validate* and *create organisation* into the installation's account; an organisation's account exports the first two as well. Tenancy, which creates tenants, is pinned to no tenant and runs beside admission in the installation's account. See [the first message](../design/tenancy/first-message.md).
- **Three commands run from a provider's account into an organisation's:** the operator's *approve agreement*, *set quota* and *request offboarding*, over the agreement's wiring, authorised there against the duty; from the installation's, *request offboarding* alone. Every other command runs from an organisation's contexts outward. Events and entries run both ways, all of them rows of [the integration contract](integration-contract.md). To be raised when the export wiring is designed.
- ***Provider* is a role in an agreement, not a kind of organisation or tenant.** Any organisation may offer products to others under agreements, by announcing into the catalogue, or stop; the provider-side contexts are pinned to the tenant of whichever organisation is the provider, and a tenant carries no kind. In this story two organisations offer: the installation's, whose product, the control plane, is listed as a given, and one created at the front desk like any other, and how it came to announce is a story of its own.
- **Only the account tells the installation's organisation apart from a provider.** It owns the installation's account and the front desk, runs the identity provider and the messaging system, and provides the control plane under the agreement every organisation holds from its creation; it may provide other products too. Running the transport, it is what makes every record append-only above the party that keeps it, which is neutrality for every provider's record and none for its own, the limit [the audit decision](audit.md) states. The messaging system's own word for the role is *operator*, which the design does not use.
