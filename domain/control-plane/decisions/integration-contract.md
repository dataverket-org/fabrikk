# The integration contract: events, commands and published state per context

*Part of [the design decisions](README.md).*

## Context

Stack's rule is that a domain event is a fact an aggregate records for its own module, and an integration event is the published contract: named after the emitting aggregate, versioned, carrying what consumers need so they don't reach back. [The audit decision](audit.md) made the commands between contexts audited too, so they belong in the same list. The crossing tables in [the map](../map/README.md) are the raw material.

## Decision

Three rules and two tables.

1. **Naming.** Fabrikk's convention holds: an event type is context, aggregate, verb in the past tense; a command is context, aggregate, verb in the imperative. The emitting context owns the name. Spelling of multi-word verbs belongs to fabrikk's subject-naming ADR.
2. **Versioning.** The type name carries no version. The payload's schema is named in the CloudEvents `dataschema` attribute and changes only additively. A change a consumer can't ignore is a new type, and the old one keeps publishing until no consumer imports it, which is checkable since every import is declared.
3. **Payload.** Every integration event and command carries an identity of its own, so a reader can tell a second delivery from a new fact, the identities of what it concerns and the organisation it concerns, and the fact in its name. A fact that concerns several organisations is published once per organisation, each copy naming that organisation alone; the full list stays with the context that owns the fact. A fact that concerns no organisation, a catalogue fact, is published once and read by all. The rarely changing facts that [reads](reads.md) projects, organisation state and quota and catalogue entries, carry their full current state so a projection can be rebuilt from the latest one. Everything else is a notification with ids; a consumer that needs more keeps a projection.

Commands from a human through the API are commands too, and are audited: their envelopes are an export of the organisation's account to Audit, listed in the second table. Their payloads are the API's design, not a crossing, and are not listed here.

Published state crosses too:

| Published state that crosses | From | To |
|---|---|---|
| The agreement entry: its state, the conditions unmet while held, per product its terms, state, quota and the day its notice ends while offboarding, and for the installation's the pending closing and the notice's end; one per agreement | Organisation, in each organisation's account | A product's contexts, each reading its own product's key; the installation's account for the installation's, whose product has no contexts to read it |
| The catalogue entry, each product with its counts, its command classes and its terms; the control plane's with its name and its terms alone | Catalogue, in the installation's account | The front desk; every organisation's Organisation; a product's contexts, in every organisation's account and every provider's; the website and aggregators |
| The audit view, every entry about an organisation, delivered as it happens | Each Audit, in its own account | That organisation's account, and wherever it takes it |
| Every command and integration event of a provider's own | Each provider's account | The installation's Audit |
| The tenants entry | Tenancy, in the installation's account | Admission, in the same account; crosses nothing |
| The installation organisation's policy, the part every organisation and every visitor reads: what the front desk requires, the limit on organisations a person may be an owner in, and the two dormancy periods | The installation's Organisation | Every organisation's account and the front desk |
| The envelopes of every command sent inside an organisation's account, a human's included, with their outcomes; never the payload | Each organisation's account, and the front desk | The Audit of the agreement the command's lifecycle belongs to: the installation's for the organisation's own lifecycle, which is the installation agreement's, and for the front desk's; each provider's for its agreement's and its products' |

Everything on the board not in the right-hand column of the first table, and not an entry in the second, is a domain event inside its context, unaudited and invisible to other contexts.

*Published language* is Evans' name for a context that exposes a documented, shared format anyone may read without a negotiated relationship, as an RSS feed or an OpenAPI document does. Catalogue is one: the same shape is read by a product's acceptance, other providers, an aggregator and the website. If readers later want to be told of changes rather than read, Catalogue gains events; that is a later choice, not a different relationship.

## Consequences

- Each context's design starts from its row: the commands it handles, the events it publishes, and the imports it declares for the events it consumes.
- The CloudEvents profile and the subject taxonomy in fabrikk follow from the list and from rule 1; they are the next design artefacts after the first context.
- The two tables together are the export list. The audit surface is the first table plus every human command through the API, which the tables do not list.
- A new product adds its rows here and answers [the seam](../../method/seam.md); the control plane's rows do not change, since a product reports to the control plane with the control plane's commands and is told in its words, see [the seam decision](seam-languages.md).
