# Commands

*Part of [Organisation's design](README.md).* A command is what a human or another context asks Organisation to do; the event behind each is the fact on the board in [the timeline](../../story/timeline/README.md). Commands from a human arrive through the API and are audited as commands, envelope and outcome only, never the payload. Authorisation reads a user's roles as they apply: a role held and not applying, because the organisation policy requires a validated login for it and the user's login is not validated, refuses the user's commands in that role until *Login validated* arrives.

## The organisation

| Command | Who sends it | Rules | Event behind it |
|---|---|---|---|
| *create organisation* | A visitor at the installation's front desk whose login has accepted the installation's current terms and is validated; it reaches Organisation through Tenancy, which creates the tenant and its account first ([the first message](../tenancy/first-message.md)) | Creates three things together: the organisation; its first owner, a user of the visitor's login, tied to the person by the person id the command carries; and the agreement with the installation, holding one product, the control plane, under the terms whose identity the command carries. Both identities were bound onto the command at entry from Person's facts, never written by the sender. The agreement activates as the installation's policy names its conditions, which today are none, so at once | Organisation created; Terms accepted and Agreement activated for the installation's agreement |
| *onboard user* | An owner, naming the login | Any login, with users elsewhere or not; the user exists with no role. Nothing is asked of the login: it need not have logged in anywhere, and it is covered by the organisation's agreement. Person is told, and answers *Login validated* if the login is validated already | User onboarded |
| *grant role* | An owner | Refused only for the owner role to a user whose login is not validated. Any other role is granted to any user and applies only while the organisation policy's requirement for it is met | Role granted to user |
| *revoke role* | An owner | The last enabled owner's role cannot be revoked | Role revoked from user |
| *disable user*, *delete user* | An owner | Which one on departure the organisation policy decides; never the last enabled owner. A disabled user holds no role that applies. Person is told either way | User disabled, User deleted |
| *set policy* | An owner | Written in the system's vocabulary, see [who's who](../../language/who.md). Never refused for the users that exist: a requirement it comes to make holds a role without its effect until the login validates, and a loosening makes every held role apply at once. The closing rule may change at any time, and a pending request is re-evaluated against it. For a provider, the conditions for activation it names are read against every held agreement with it, and a condition dropped activates what waited for it alone; a condition named later never deactivates. A rule about what the organisation holds names a product the catalogue lists or every product, and never a product's own command | Policy set |

There is no command that deletes an organisation: the owners close one with *request offboarding* for the agreement with the installation, below.

## The agreement with a provider

An organisation holds one agreement per provider, and the agreement with the installation from its creation; the commands are the same for every agreement, and where the installation's differs the row says so.

| Command | Who sends it | Rules | Event behind it |
|---|---|---|---|
| *accept terms* | An owner, naming the provider, a product and its terms | Refused unless the catalogue lists the product under the provider with those terms, current or pending, and not withdrawn unless the organisation holds it and is accepting its new terms. The first at a provider creates the agreement, held until every condition for activation the provider's policy names is met, and Tenancy wires the account to the provider's. The first for a product adds it to the agreement and wires the account for it. One naming a product already taken accepts its new terms. Refused for a product that is offboarding; one naming a product whose terms have ended takes it again from the start, the quota from the defaults. For the installation's agreement it only ever accepts new terms, the control plane being held from birth | Terms accepted |
| *approve agreement* | The provider's operator, over the agreement's wiring | Meets the condition *approval*. Refused unless the agreement is held. The agreement activates on it if no other condition is unmet, which is the activation policy's doing | Agreement approved; Agreement activated by policy |
| *set quota* | The provider's operator | Per product the organisation has taken, in the counts the product's catalogue entry names; refused for a count the entry does not name. Until it is sent the quota is the announcement's default, so a provider that wants to look at each customer announces a default of zero. One number per count, the provider's: a customer's own cap is a budget, not a ceiling, and no story has asked for one. The control plane names no counts | Quota set |
| *request offboarding* | An owner, by choice, for a product or an agreement; or the provider's operator under the terms, with a reason: declined, dormancy, terms expired or product withdrawn | Carries the terms' notice for each product named, whoever sends it, held on the product's entry and carried on the event. Each product named refuses new asks and releases reservations at once; the editors give back within the notice; at its end the product ends what is still held. The terms end on the product's *Organisation cleared*, whenever it arrives, so the notice is an upper bound and never a wait. Refused for a product not taken or already offboarding. **Declined:** the operator's no to a held agreement, allowed only while a condition for activation is unmet; the agreement ends at once with no notice, nothing having been held. **The installation's agreement:** an owner's request is how the organisation closes, refused while any other agreement stands; under the policy's closing rule it is offboarding at once under *any owner*, and under *every enabled owner* the first request is pending on the agreement, the other owners told, and complete when every enabled owner has requested. The installation's operator requests it for dormancy, with the installation's notice, which it cannot withdraw, or for terms expired. No *Organisation cleared* crosses for the control plane, which holds nothing but the organisation itself: its terms end at once on the owners' complete request, and at the end of notice on the operator's | Offboarding requested, carrying the sender, the reason, the products and the day each one's notice ends |
| *withdraw offboarding* | An owner, for the installation's agreement | Withdraws the owner's own request from a pending closing under the every-owner rule; refused for an owner not named on it and once the request is complete. A pending request with no owner left is gone. No other agreement has a pending request, so the command exists for the installation's alone | Offboarding withdrawn |

## Policies, not commands

These happen on the board without anyone asking, and are designed in [policies](policies.md): *Agreement activated* when every condition for activation is met; *Terms replaced* and *Terms expired* on Catalogue's new terms and their date; *Offboarding lapsed* when a provider's terms are accepted during the installation's dormancy notice; *Terms ended* on *Organisation cleared*, or by Organisation's own hand for the control plane and for a declined agreement, and *Agreement ended* once every product's terms have ended; *User deleted* for every user, then *Organisation deleted*, after the last *Agreement ended*.
